Artisk

Applying a new liquid-glass brand system to two text-heavy pages — FAQ and About — without trading away readability.

RoleUX / Visual Design
TeamTeam of 10+
TimelineOct 25–Mar 26
ToolsFigma, Nano Banana
Problem statement

Brand without breaking two text-heavy pages

Artisk is a company that builds AI-empowered design tools for brands. It rebranded from a loud, high-contrast look to a softer liquid-glass visual language built on translucency and depth. As part of the team redesign, I brought two text-heavy pages — FAQ and About — onto the new system.

Two pages, two jobs

FAQ and About are task pages: one is where a user solves a specific question without contacting support, the other is where a user reads to decide whether to trust the product. Whatever the brand layer looked like, it couldn't get in the way of those two jobs.

Insight

Readability needs to be improved

The neon-on-black look was high-contrast by default, so the About page's uniform wall of text still felt legible. Moving to a softer material removed that crutch and exposed the real issue: the information had no hierarchy, and nothing guided the eye.

Process

Auditing each page against its job

I audited both pages against what each is for — the FAQ against scannability, the About page against readability — then treated the glass material as a layering tool rather than a skin, deciding where it could earn its place and where it had to stay off the text. In parallel, I produced the banner imagery as one coordinated AI-generated set spanning both pages.

Key decisions

Three decisions across two pages

FAQ: brand applied without breaking scannability

FAQ before / after

The FAQ already had its structure — search, categories, an accordion of answers. My work was bringing it onto the new visual system while protecting how scannable it was: keeping glass on the structural layer (search, category rail, cards) and answers on solid, high-contrast surfaces, so a user could still scan straight to the one question they came with.

About: same information, made readable

About before / after

I didn't change what the page says; I changed how it's represented. Same information, rebuilt around a clear visual hierarchy — paced sections, a reading rhythm, defined weight — so a block people skim past became something they can actually read.

One visual system across both pages

AI banner set (FAQ + About)

I created the banner imagery as one coordinated AI-generated set, so both pages open with a consistent signature and each gets an entry point that anchors the content. The same rule held throughout: translucency lowers text contrast, so glass carries depth and structure, never body copy.

Reflection & next steps

Execution within an existing structure

This was a team redesign, so my focus was execution within an existing structure — applying the brand system and protecting readability, not rebuilding the pages' architecture. Taking it further, the honest next steps are a contrast and accessibility audit (glass on text-heavy pages puts legibility most at risk) and testing on lower-end screens and in bright light, where translucency degrades most.
