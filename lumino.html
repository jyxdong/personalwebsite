Lumino

Context

What is Lumino?

Lumino is a smart mushroom-shaped nightlight and companion app that supports young children through the transition to sleeping on their own. The nightlight offers soothing light to help a child self-settle, with a built-in speaker and mic that play parent-selected audio and enable a two-way intercom. Through the app, parents control the light and intercom and select or upload stories and audio.

Problem statement

Sleeping alone is a milestone for the whole family

A child moves out of co-sleeping and is about to sleep alone. At first they wake at night, call out, or need a parent nearby to settle back down. For the child, the dark room feels unsafe without a parent close by. For the parent, the repeated check-ins and drifting bedtimes mean lost sleep that follows them into the next day. This in-between stretch, no longer co-sleeping but not yet independent, is where it feels hardest for both.

What parents told us

We surveyed eight parents with a young child under five. Their answers backed up the secondary research: bedtime is relatively stressful for them, and they are open to an outside device helping with it.

    Insight 1

    Replacement of parent needed

    Children are afraid of the dark and of being alone — what they need most is their parent's presence. But parents reported re-entering the room one to two times a night, describing bedtime as moderately stressful. A replacement for parent presence is needed.

    Insight 2

    Operation with simplicity

    Targeting 5-year-olds, every interaction had to be tactile and single step. Anything harder to operate in the dark risks becoming its own source of frustration, undermining the sense of security the device is meant to give.

    System and User

    A digital and physical combined experience

    Screen-free

    Our users are 4 to 6, so the child's side stays fully tangible: a light to pat and two buttons to press, with no screen at bedtime.

    Accessible

    It sits within arm's reach of the bed, so a child can work it alone in the dark. Parents reach the same controls through the app from wherever they are.

    Emotional

    The light and a parent's own recorded voice keep the child company. The parent settles them without walking back in, so both sides get through the night.

    Physical Design

    Exploring the form

    With the direction set, we sketched broadly to explore form, proportion, and how a child might physically interact with the device before narrowing down.

    Two directions emerged

    From this range of early ideas, two directions emerged, each answering the brief differently: a simple mushroom nightlight, and a more playful glass house.

    Mushroom Nightlight

    A familiar form the child can bond with: a soft-glowing top and two buttons, one for a story and one to reach a parent. Safe and simple enough for a child to use alone.

    Glass House

    A playful, narrative concept: placing a magnetic figurine inside the translucent house makes it glow, while a small screen inside plays a gentle night scene to follow.

    Challenge

    From conflict to a clear direction

    My teammate and I split on which direction to build. They were drawn to the glass house's storytelling, while I worried it asked too much of our users. Rather than argue preference, we evaluated both against who we were designing for.

    Weighed against our users, the glass house raised three concerns: the interaction was too complex for a 3–6 year old alone in the dark, glass raised real safety worries at bedtime, and the animated scene risked engaging the child instead of settling them to sleep.

    Grounding the decision in the user, not our preferences, gave us a shared way to converge. We committed to the mushroom and brought it into 3D.

    Final 3D Design

    Intercom Button

    Keep pressing to activate intercom feature: hold to talk. The button is sized with a length of 1.8 inch for toddlers' small hands.

    Play Button

    Press to start playing the preset story/audio playlist. Size is differentiated from the intercom button so that it can be distinguished by touch in the dark.

    Silicone Top

    The tap-to-activate silicone top is designed to suit toddlers' limited fine motor control. An open-palm pat is instinctive, and its soft and smooth texture prevents potential harm in darkness.

    Interface Development

    Bringing the app’s content into focus

    After exploring several interface directions, we chose to place graphic representations of the app’s content at the center of each screen. This created a clearer visual focus, made the controls easier to scan, and gave Lumino a friendlier, more cohesive character.

    01 · Sketch

    Three ways in

    We sketched three directions for the parent app: a list of control cards (A), a layout built around the nightlight itself (B), and a scene picker (C).

    02 · Wireframe

    Same controls, three structures

    Each sketch became a mid-fi wireframe holding the same controls, brightness, rhythm, volume, timer and story, so we could compare how each one organised them.

    03 · Hi-fi

    Two directions forward

    The scene picker added a step parents didn't need at bedtime, so we set it aside. The control list went to hi-fi as it was, and the device layout got two takes: the nightlight alone, and with controls built into it.

    04 · Final

    The content in focus

    We kept the controls on the device: brightness lives in the lamp, volume in its base. With the nightlight at the centre, each screen has a clear focus and Lumino a friendlier character.

    Testing

    Putting the prototype in front of a parent

    We ran a moderated usability test with parents of a four-year-old, using four task scenarios and a SUS-style rating. Every task was completed, so the structure held.

    Device for kids, app for parents

    Parents liked the split: the child stays off screens while they stay in control from another room, as they already do.

    A form that felt safe by the bed

    Parents found the mushroom warm and approachable, and were happy to keep it by the bed. It read as friendly, not tech.

    Every task done without any help

    Parents set up stories, adjusted the light, and reached their child without confusion, even at the end of a long day.

    Improvements on function details

    Parents also pointed out a few small gaps. Based on their suggestions, we refined these UI and function details.

    Content below the fold unseen

    The main screen didn't signal that more content was available, so it went unseen until prompted.

    Preview story is needed

    There was no way to know what a story contained before it reached the child. Parents need to preview the story contents first before adding them to a playlist.

    No device management

    Given the possibility that users may purchase multiple devices or change devices, there should be a section in settings to delete or connect devices.

    Reflection + Next Steps

    How can Lumino be more comforting?

    Mapping the Flow Earlier

    Next time I'd map how child and parent move between device and app before any screen, so the physical and digital split follows the flow.

    Testing in Real Context

    The next step is testing within parents' actual evening routine rather than a neutral setting, since context does half the work here.

    Multi-Child Homes

    Device management opens the way to households with more than one child and more than one Lumino, which the app could grow to support.
