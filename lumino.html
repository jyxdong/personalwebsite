Lumino

A companion that guides young children to sleep on their own.

Team & RoleGinny Dong: UX Research and Interface Design
Kylinn Ding: CAD Design and Usability Testing
TimelineMar 2026 - Apr 2026
ToolsFigma, Onshape
Context

What is Lumino?

Lumino is a smart mushroom-shaped nightlight and companion app that supports young children through the transition to sleeping on their own. The nightlight offers soothing light to help a child self-settle, with a built-in speaker and mic that play parent-selected audio and enable a two-way intercom. Through the app, parents control the light and intercom and select or upload stories and audio.

Problem statement

Kids cannot sleep alone at night

A child moves out of co-sleeping and is about to sleep alone. At first they wake at night, call out, and a parent walks back in. Then it happens again. On a normal night this cycle repeats one to two times, sometimes more, and both sides end up depleted.

Most products built for this moment are designed for the parent: monitors, trackers, routine apps. We wanted to build a product that connects the child with the parent while making this stage easier for both.

Target Users

Primary users:

Kids that fall within age 4-6, entering the stage of sleeping alone in their own room.

Secondary users:

Parents who have young children and want a second channel that helps keep their child's sleep more secure.

Insight 1

Replacement of parent needed

Children are afraid of the dark and of being alone — what they need most is their parent's presence. But parents reported re-entering the room one to two times a night, describing bedtime as moderately stressful. A replacement for parent presence is needed.

Insight 2

Operation with simplicity

Targeting 5-year-olds, every interaction had to be tactile and single step. Anything harder to operate in the dark risks becoming its own source of frustration, undermining the sense of security the device is meant to give.

System and User

A digital and physical combined experience

Why a physical product?

Our target users are 4- to 6-year-olds, so we wanted the experience to stay fully tangible and away from digital distractions.

Why an app for control?

So parents can access the device wherever they are — a companion app is more portable than the device itself.

Key decisions

Two decisions, one goal: comfort in the dark

How the light behaves, and how the buttons are found, are what make the device usable by a child who can't turn on the room light.

The light fades as the child settles

Breathing-light rhythm

A nightlight that stays at a constant brightness solves one problem and creates another. Lumino's light pulses in a slow inhale-and-exhale rhythm that a child's breathing naturally follows, and overall brightness decreases as the cycle continues, so the room is dimmest by the time the child is closest to sleep. White noise can play alongside it, so the auditory and visual cues work together instead of competing for attention.

Buttons a child can find in the dark

Button shape + size

The story button and the call button are shaped differently and placed on opposite sides, so a child learns them by feel within a few nights. Sizing came from pediatric anthropometric data: toddler fingertip breadth of roughly 0.4 to 0.6 inches set the minimum button size, and the top surface was sized for partial palm contact so an open-handed pat works without precise aim. Actuation resistance is high enough that rolling over in sleep won't trigger anything.

Testing

Putting the prototype in front of a parent

We ran a moderated usability test of the app prototype with several parents of a four-year-old, using four task scenarios and a SUS-style rating. All tasks were completed, which told us the structure held. One walkthrough was recorded. Three specific interactions did not.

Content below the fold unseen

The main screen didn't signal that more content was available, so it went unseen until prompted.

Preview story is needed

There was no way to know what a story contained before it reached the child. Parents need to preview the story contents first before adding them to a playlist.

No device management

Given the possibility that users may purchase multiple devices or change devices, there should be a section in settings to delete or connect devices.

Reflection + Next Steps

How can Lumino be more comforting?

Mapping the Flow Earlier

Next time I'd map how the child and parent interact across the device and app before committing to any interface, so the split between physical and digital is grounded in the flow.

Testing in Real Context

The next step is testing within parents' actual evening routine rather than a neutral setting, since context does half the work here.

Extending to Multiple Children

The device management work opened up to support households with more than one child, and more than one Lumino. Given more time the interfaces can be further designed to support more Lumino Devices.
